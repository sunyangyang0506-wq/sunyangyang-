# Teach English in China — Job Finder

Find English teaching jobs in China or teach online from abroad with China-linked providers. Compare pay, requirements and checked application routes.

Chinese name: 去中国教英语｜岗位检索.

## Start with one choice

When the route is unknown, ask exactly one question in plain English and wait for the choice:

**Would you like to teach English online from abroad, in person in China, or both?**

Accept a number (1, 2, 3 in that order), words, or a reply in any language. If the route is already clear, go straight to the jobs. If the reader says “anything,” use both. On a scheduled run use the saved choice. Deliver the brief in English unless another language is requested.

Show jobs immediately after the choice. Use preferences volunteered in ordinary conversation. Leave location, nationality, qualifications, learner age, experience, availability, arrival date and contact details out of the intake. Report each employer's conditions beside the job so the reader can decide. Do not infer nationality, English proficiency, eligibility or a right to work from someone's language. A reader can request explanations, a credential provider, or application support later.

## Find openings

Use [the source guide](references/sources.md) to choose different sources for each selected route. Search in English and Chinese. For online work, establish a China connection (the educator, client or students) and that the teacher can deliver the entire job from abroad. Distinguish a China-based platform serving global learners from a role teaching students in mainland China. Exclude office-based online delivery. Record any country or time zone restrictions from the listing. Prioritize adult or corporate English when searching for cross-border classes. If an overseas tutoring listing involves school-age mainland students, check the current official rules for the actual provider, curriculum and learner location. Withhold opportunities that conflict with the applicable rules or whose scope cannot be established.

For in-person work, search universities, schools and education providers recruiting for a named mainland campus or placement. Label talent pools and multischool intake as such. Prioritize dated individual vacancies. Note a requirement to be in China already. When work permit support matters, report the employer's wording; do not infer sponsorship or legal eligibility.

## Verify the reader's path

Open the individual job, check its deadline, and follow the application link to the form, or find the hiring email expressly named in the posting. Do not submit. Record the date checked. Give the job title, provider, city or allowed remote locations, learner group, pay with its original unit, one or two key entry conditions, and a specific next action. Use “not listed” for missing details. Avoid promoting an upper pay bound to a guaranteed rate. If a known hard requirement conflicts with a volunteered preference, omit the job.

Check the page a reader outside China would actually need. Record the language, redirects and any visible account, +86 phone, WeChat, QR or regional access requirements. Distinguish an opened job page from an opened form and from successful submission. A search snippet, English page, .cn/.com suffix or domestic page check does not establish country-specific access. Claim “tested from [country]” only with a documented test from that country and date, identifying whether it reached the listing or the form. A failed check is inconclusive about other locations.

Publish each role with two distinct, usable links. **Primary** goes to the English job posting with application instructions or its form. **Backup** goes to the same opening on a separate host with usable instructions, or to a clickable hiring email explicitly designated for this vacancy. An official email for requesting the form counts when labelled accurately. On both routes check the final destination and describe what the reader can do there. For email, verify the printed address and purpose against the employer posting, without sending. A role description that sends the reader back to the inaccessible primary route cannot serve as a backup.

Match the employer, job reference or title, location and deadline across routes. Reject tracking variants, links that resolve to the same page or depend on the same login, generic homepages and generic talent-pool forms for specific vacancies. If an observed local-phone or app-only barrier makes an entry unusable from abroad, replace it. If two suitable routes cannot be verified, hold the role for a future issue. Make these checks without asking the reader for a country. When no country-specific test was run, describe the route observed, without promising worldwide access.

## Write today's brief

Use [the English daily brief](assets/daily-brief.md). See the [dated example](references/sample-brief.md) for a school email fallback and an online search with no publishable result. Recheck the live sources for every new brief. Keep only the route(s) chosen. Use short job cards, sorted by relevance and closing date. Put the application link or official application email beside each role. Skip outbound marketing and unsolicited credential referrals.

For the first issue, show current openings. For repeat issues, compare the employer's job ID or canonical posting URL with prior issues for this reader. Use employer + title + city/remote area when sources duplicate a role. Include newly found jobs and material changes such as a reopened application, new rate or deadline. Label evergreen pools. Do not call a newly discovered role newly posted. Suppress duplicates and expired jobs. If no qualifying changes exist, report that in one sentence with the sources checked. If search failed or history is unavailable, say so and distinguish an incomplete check from zero openings. On an ordinary successful day, end with a short line naming the sources actually checked.

## Deliver when requested

Return an on-demand brief in the conversation. When a reader asks to receive it daily, reuse their selected track. Use the host's scheduler and supported delivery channel. Use the current conversation for “send it to me here”; use an explicit destination for another channel. Resolve only missing delivery details needed for scheduling, such as local time/zone or recipient, after showing jobs. Keep preferences and delivery history in the reader's private task context. Never store subscriber information in this skill or its public repository.

Look up existing schedules for the same reader, route and destination. Update that subscription when appropriate. Keep unrelated readers' tasks separate. Persist the route, local schedule and destination in the task, with these run instructions: reload this skill, use the source guide, recheck the application routes, compare with the last delivered issue and deliver today's English brief. Use the local issue date and destination to guard against duplicate sends. Report a schedule or delivery as successful only when the service confirms it. If scheduled delivery, history or a requested channel is unavailable, return the brief here and explain the exact delivery limitation. Installing this skill creates no subscription by itself.
